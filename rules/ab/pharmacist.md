# AB – Pharmacist: renewals and many minor problems

## What Primary Care Alberta says
- Pharmacies provide medications, health advice and services like immunizations, prescription refills and medication reviews, health screenings, quitting-smoking advice and help managing chronic conditions.

## When this route fits
- Prescription renewal of a medicine the person already takes. All Alberta pharmacists can renew most existing prescriptions, and change the dose, form or length.
- Minor, non-urgent problems. Pharmacists with Additional Prescribing Authorization (APA) can assess and start many new prescriptions.
- The assessment is covered for Alberta residents with a valid health card.

## What Alberta pharmacists can help with
Source: Navigate Care Alberta, "The Alberta Pharmacist Advantage" (independent directory citing the Alberta College of Pharmacy).
Some items need a pharmacist with Additional Prescribing Authorization (APA) – call ahead and ask.
When the user's own description matches an item, you may say that **pharmacists in Alberta can help with that kind of problem**
(service scope), e.g. "In Alberta, pharmacists can assess urinary tract infection symptoms and prescribe if needed."
Never tell the user they have the condition.

- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs)** – Pharmacists can assess and prescribe antibiotics for uncomplicated UTIs in adults. *Who:* Adults 18+ with typical UTI symptoms
- **Cold Sores (Herpes Labialis)** – Antiviral creams and oral medications prescribed directly. Best started within 72 hours. *Who:* Anyone with a recurring cold sore
- **Minor Acne** – Topical treatments for mild to moderate acne. *Who:* Mild-moderate acne not responding to OTC products
- **Hay Fever & Seasonal Allergies** – Prescription-strength antihistamines and nasal sprays available directly. *Who:* Anyone with seasonal allergy symptoms
- **Tick Bites (Lyme Prophylaxis)** – Preventive antibiotic (doxycycline) to reduce Lyme disease risk. Time-sensitive. *Who:* Anyone bitten by a tick within 72 hours
- **Prescription Renewals** – Pharmacists can renew or extend most prescriptions to keep you on track. *Who:* Anyone on an ongoing prescription needing renewal
- **Pink Eye (Conjunctivitis)** – Antibiotic eye drops for bacterial conjunctivitis. *Who:* Adults and children with eye redness and discharge
- **Heartburn & Acid Reflux (GERD)** – Proton pump inhibitors and other medications for persistent heartburn. *Who:* Adults with recurring heartburn
- **Menstrual Cramps** – Stronger pain relief when OTC options aren't enough. *Who:* Anyone with moderate to severe menstrual pain
- **Oral Thrush** – Antifungal treatments for oral thrush. *Who:* Adults with white patches or soreness in the mouth
- **Diaper Rash** – Medicated creams for moderate diaper rash. *Who:* Infants with persistent diaper rash
- **Mild Eczema & Dermatitis** – Topical corticosteroids for mild eczema flare-ups. *Who:* Anyone with mild, non-infected eczema
- **Hemorrhoids** – Prescription-strength creams and suppositories. *Who:* Adults with mild to moderate symptoms
- **Smoking Cessation** – NRT, varenicline, and stop-smoking aids. Many offer free counselling. *Who:* Anyone looking to quit smoking or vaping
- **Contraception** – Hormonal contraceptives and emergency contraception. Confidential. *Who:* Anyone seeking birth control
- **Travel Health** – Travel medications and vaccines at many pharmacies. *Who:* Anyone planning international travel
- **Vaginal Yeast Infections** – Antifungal treatments for uncomplicated vaginal yeast infections. *Who:* Adults with typical yeast infection symptoms
- **Shingles (Herpes Zoster)** – Antiviral treatment for shingles. Most effective when started within 72 hours of rash onset. *Who:* Adults with characteristic shingles rash
- **Impetigo** – Topical or oral antibiotics for this common bacterial skin infection. *Who:* Children and adults with crusted skin sores
- **Insect Bites & Stings** – Treatment for allergic reactions and infections from insect bites. *Who:* Anyone with a symptomatic bite or sting (not anaphylaxis — call 911)
- **Nausea/Vomiting in Pregnancy** – Safe anti-nausea medications during pregnancy. *Who:* Pregnant individuals with morning sickness
- **Pinworms** – Antiparasitic treatment for pinworm infections. *Who:* Children and adults with confirmed or suspected pinworms
- **Musculoskeletal Sprains** – Anti-inflammatory medications and pain management for sprains. *Who:* Anyone with a minor sprain or strain
- **Oral Ulcers (Canker Sores)** – Prescription treatments for painful mouth ulcers. *Who:* Anyone with recurring or painful canker sores
- **Warts (non-facial, non-genital)** – Prescription-strength treatments for common and plantar warts. *Who:* Anyone with warts (excluding face and genital areas)
- **COVID-19 Treatment** – Antiviral treatment (e.g., Paxlovid) for eligible patients with COVID-19. *Who:* Patients at higher risk of severe COVID-19 outcomes
- **Diabetes Management** – APA pharmacists can initiate and adjust diabetes medications, order A1c and glucose tests, and provide ongoing therapy management. *Who:* Adults with Type 2 diabetes — especially those needing medication adjustments between doctor visits
- **Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)** – APA pharmacists can prescribe and adjust blood pressure medications and monitor your response. *Who:* Adults with diagnosed hypertension needing medication management
- **Insomnia & Sleep Concerns** – Short-term sleep medications and non-medication strategies. *Who:* Adults with short-term insomnia (pharmacist will screen for underlying causes)
- **Gonorrhea & Chlamydia (STI Testing & Treatment)** – Pharmacists with APA and PRAC-ID can order STI testing and initiate treatment for positive results. Confidential. *Who:* Adults seeking STI screening — no doctor visit required
- **Strep Throat & Acute Infections** – APA pharmacists can assess, test (where point-of-care testing is available), and prescribe antibiotics for common acute infections. *Who:* Anyone with symptoms of bacterial infection
- **Skin Infections (Cellulitis, Abscesses)** – APA pharmacists can assess and prescribe oral antibiotics for uncomplicated skin infections. *Who:* Anyone with redness, swelling, warmth, or pus at a wound or skin area
- **Ear Infections (Otitis Media / Externa)** – Antibiotic ear drops or oral antibiotics depending on the type of infection. *Who:* Adults and children with ear pain and signs of infection
- **Upper Respiratory Infections** – Assessment and treatment for sinus infections, bronchitis, and persistent coughs that may need prescription treatment. *Who:* Anyone with a cold or cough lasting longer than expected or worsening

Problems NOT on this list (for example constipation, diarrhea, cough lasting weeks): a pharmacist can still give advice and
suggest over-the-counter products, but if there are other symptoms or it is not getting better, offer virtual care or a walk-in clinic as well.

## When this route does NOT fit (offer another option)
- The problem is not on the list above and comes with other symptoms, is new and unexplained, or is not getting better → virtual care or walk-in.
- The pharmacist assesses and says it needs a doctor (they refer to a walk-in clinic or doctor).
- Narcotics and controlled substances, medicines that need regular bloodwork monitoring, or complex situations. Pharmacists may refuse or give only a very short supply; they will refer to a walk-in clinic or doctor.
- Anything that feels urgent or is getting worse quickly → 811 or walk-in / urgent care.

## What to do
- Call ahead and ask: "Is a pharmacist with additional prescribing authorization (APA) working today?"
- Bring: Alberta Health Care card, the medicine bottle or a list of medicines (MyHealth Records screenshot works).
