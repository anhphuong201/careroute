# BC – Pharmacist: renewals, emergency supply and 21 minor ailments

## When this route fits
- Prescription renewal when ALL of these are true: the medicine has run out with no refills left; the person is stable on the same medicine and dose (3–6 months or longer, no concerns); the prescription was written in the past 24 months.
- Ran out of an urgently needed medicine → a pharmacy can give a short emergency supply.
- Minor ailments: pharmacists can assess and prescribe for 21 minor ailments (for example uncomplicated UTIs, allergies, pink eye, dermatitis, nicotine dependence) and contraception. Free for B.C. residents, in person in a pharmacy (Minor Ailment and Contraception Service).

## When this route does NOT fit
- Cancer chemotherapy; prescriptions marked "do not renew/adapt"; narcotics and controlled drugs beyond 30 days or the original amount.
- Severe symptoms or an emergency → call 9-1-1.

## What to do
- Book or walk in. A renewed prescription must be picked up at the same pharmacy.
- Bring: BC Services Card / PHN, the medicine bottle or list.
